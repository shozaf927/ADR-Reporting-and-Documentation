# Module 04 — Clinical Context and Reaction Management

## Clinical context
Capture information that may help interpret the event or identify alternative explanations:
- Relevant medical and surgical history.
- Known drug or other allergies.
- Existing comorbidities.
- Pregnancy/lactation status when relevant.
- Relevant laboratory values and dates.
- Diagnostic tests, imaging and clinical assessments.
- Prescription, OTC, herbal and supplement use.
- Relevant infection, environmental or occupational exposures.

## Management actions
Document what was actually done:
- Suspected medicine continued.
- Dose reduced.
- Treatment interrupted or withdrawn.
- Specific treatment administered for the reaction.
- Supportive care or hospital treatment.
- Specialist assessment or other intervention.

Include dates and response where available.

## Dechallenge
Dechallenge describes what happened after dose reduction or withdrawal of the suspected medicine. Record:
- Whether the product was stopped/reduced.
- Date of the action.
- Whether the reaction improved, resolved, persisted or worsened.
- Other treatments or changes occurring at the same time.

Improvement after withdrawal can support an association but does not prove it.

## Rechallenge
Record rechallenge only if re-exposure occurred as part of clinical care or is otherwise reliably documented. Include timing and whether the reaction recurred.

**Safety:** Do not deliberately re-administer a suspected medicine solely to test causality when this may expose the patient to harm. A rechallenge is not required for every report.

## Quality checks
- Are alternative causes considered?
- Are relevant tests and history captured?
- Are management actions dated?
- Is dechallenge interpreted cautiously?
- Is any rechallenge information documented without encouraging unsafe testing?
