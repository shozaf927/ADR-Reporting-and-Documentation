# Module 05 — Seriousness and Patient Outcome

## Severity versus seriousness
These terms are not interchangeable.

- **Severity** describes the intensity of a clinical manifestation (for example, mild, moderate or severe).
- **Seriousness** is a regulatory classification based on specified outcomes or medical importance.

A severe symptom is not automatically a serious adverse event, and an event that appears clinically modest may meet a seriousness criterion depending on its consequences.

## Common regulatory seriousness criteria
An event is generally considered serious if it results in any of the following:
1. Death.
2. Life-threatening event at the time of occurrence.
3. Inpatient hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation.
4. Persistent or significant disability/incapacity.
5. Congenital anomaly or birth defect.
6. Other medically important condition requiring intervention to prevent one of the listed outcomes.

Apply the definitions and reporting rules of the relevant jurisdiction and current guidance. Do not equate emergency-department attendance with inpatient hospitalisation unless the applicable criteria are met.

## Outcome fields
Record the latest known status using the reporting system's permitted terms, such as:
- Recovered/resolved.
- Recovering/resolving.
- Not recovered/not resolved.
- Recovered with sequelae.
- Fatal.
- Unknown.

Do not infer causality between the medicine and a death merely because death occurred after exposure. Record the facts and clinical assessment.

## Quality checks
- Has seriousness been assessed against each applicable criterion?
- Is the reason for seriousness documented?
- Is severity recorded separately, if requested?
- Is the latest patient outcome documented?
- Is unknown outcome distinguished from ongoing reaction?
