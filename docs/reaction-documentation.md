# Module 02 — Suspected Adverse Reaction Details

## Purpose
Create a clear, clinically meaningful and chronological account of the event.

## Required fields
- Verbatim reporter description, where useful.
- Clear clinical description of signs, symptoms and diagnosis.
- Event onset date/time, if known.
- Event end or recovery date/time, if known.
- Event duration and clinical course.
- Relevant examination, diagnostic and laboratory findings.
- Treatment provided specifically for the reaction.
- Current status at the time of reporting.

## Writing a good reaction narrative
Prefer specific clinical language over vague wording. For example, document the location and morphology of a rash, associated symptoms, progression and relevant examination findings when known. Do not convert a reporter's suspicion into a confirmed diagnosis without supporting clinical evidence.

## Chronology
Record dates consistently and distinguish:
1. First dose/exposure.
2. Dose changes.
3. First symptom or sign.
4. Medical assessment and investigations.
5. Treatment or withdrawal.
6. Improvement, resolution or latest follow-up.

If the reaction is ongoing, mark it as ongoing/not recovered as appropriate. Do not estimate a recovery date.

## Quality checks
- Is the event described clearly?
- Is onset recorded with an appropriate date precision?
- Is recovery documented only if known?
- Are event seriousness and outcome assessed separately?
- Are supporting clinical findings included where available?
